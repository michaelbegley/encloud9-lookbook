Which Dynamics 365 Customizations Are Worth It (And Which Ones We Usually Advise Against)

Have you ever opened a Dynamics 365 account record and wondered, “Do we really need all of this?”

A field gets added. Then another. Someone requests a custom form. A workflow gets built to handle a unique situation. A few years later, users are scrolling through pages of information they rarely use, and simple updates feel anything but simple. The problem isn’t Dynamics 365 customizations themselves. Some Dynamics 365 customizations can improve adoption, reporting, and productivity. The challenge is knowing which customizations add real business value and which ones simply add more complexity. After years of implementing, optimizing, and supporting Dynamics 365, we’ve seen both.

Dynamics 365 Customizations We Often Recommend

Not all Dynamics 365 customizations are bad. In fact, some can make the system significantly more useful and easier for employees to adopt.

Role-Based Forms

Different teams need different information. Sales reps don’t need to see everything that managers need, and managers don’t need to see everything that service teams use. One of the most valuable Dynamics 365 customizations is tailoring forms to specific user groups. Cleaner screens make it easier for users to find what they need and enter accurate data.

Workflow and Process Automation

When employees perform the same task over and over, automation can be a huge win. Automated lead assignment, follow-up reminders, task creation, and notifications are Dynamics 365 customizations that often deliver immediate value. The best automations eliminate repetitive work without making the process harder to follow.

Integrations That Eliminate Duplicate Data Entry

Few things frustrate users more than entering the same data into multiple systems. Dynamics 365 customizations that connect CRM data with Outlook, ERP systems, marketing platforms, or customer service applications can save time while improving data quality.

Dashboards That Support Better Decisions

A well-designed dashboard helps leaders quickly understand what’s happening in the business. Among all Dynamics 365 customizations, reporting and dashboard improvements are often some of the most valuable because they help organizations get more insight from the data they’re already collecting.

Custom Fields With a Clear Purpose

Custom fields aren’t the enemy. The key question is why the field exists. If it supports reporting, forecasting, automation, or a business process, it may be worthwhile. If there’s no clear answer, it’s probably worth reconsidering.

Dynamics 365 Customizations We Often Recommend Avoiding

These Dynamics 365 customizations tend to create more problems than they solve.

Adding Fields “Just in Case”

Most CRM administrators have heard this request: “Let’s add the field now in case we need it later.”

Unfortunately, “just in case” fields have a way of multiplying. Over time, they clutter forms, confuse users, and make data entry more frustrating.

Recreating Your Old CRM

One of the biggest mistakes we see is treating Dynamics 365 as an opportunity to rebuild an old CRM system feature-for-feature. A new CRM implementation should improve processes, not preserve every habit from the past. Often, the most effective Dynamics 365 customizations simplify the user experience rather than replicate legacy systems.

Customizing Around a Broken Process

Technology can’t fix a broken process. Before requesting new Dynamics 365 customizations, ask whether the underlying process is working. Otherwise, you may end up automating inefficiencies instead of solving them.

Complex Custom Code Without a Strong Business Case

Custom code has its place, but it comes with long-term maintenance costs. Whenever standard functionality, configuration, or low-code tools can meet the requirement, that’s often the better long-term choice. Every custom development project should have a clear business justification.

Approval Processes for Everything

Approvals can improve accountability, but they can also create bottlenecks. We’ve seen organizations build complex approval chains that slow down day-to-day work and frustrate users. The goal should be control where it’s needed, not approval for every single action.

How to Evaluate Dynamics 365 Customizations Before Building Them

Before approving any Dynamics 365 customization, ask:

  • What business problem does this solve?
  • How many users will benefit?
  • Can standard functionality accomplish the same thing?
  • Who will maintain it over time?
  • Will this still make sense three years from now?

If those questions are difficult to answer, the customization may not be necessary.

The Bottom Line on Dynamics 365 Customizations

The best Dynamics 365 environments aren’t usually the most customized. They’re the ones where every field, workflow, form, integration, and report serves a clear purpose.

We’ve worked with organizations that struggled because they didn’t customize enough. We’ve worked with many more that struggled because years of well-intentioned Dynamics 365 customizations created systems that were harder to use, harder to maintain, and harder to trust.

The goal isn’t to avoid Dynamics 365 customizations. The goal is to make thoughtful decisions that improve the user experience, support business goals, and keep the system manageable as your organization grows.
