What does a Dataverse storage warning mean?

Have you received a Microsoft Dataverse storage notification? Maybe you received a Dataverse storage warning and thought, “We’ll deal with that later.” You’re not alone. Most companies don’t spend much time thinking about Microsoft Dataverse storage until Microsoft warns them that they are at 85% of their allocation. By then, the issue may have been growing for months, sometimes years, without anyone realizing it.

The good news is that these notifications give you an opportunity to get ahead of the problem. The bad news is that ignoring them could create challenges for your Dynamics 365 and Power Platform environments down the road.

Microsoft Dataverse sits at the heart of Dynamics 365 and Power Platform environments. It has three kinds of storage: database, file and log. Microsoft sends the first warning when any of the three has less than 15% left, the next under 5%, and the last when you go over. Admins and makers get the emails every week, and a banner shows in the Power Platform admin center and your Dynamics 365 apps.

What is using up your Dataverse storage?

Every customer record, attachment, workflow, integration, audit log, and business process adds to your Dataverse storage usage. The problem is that storage growth isn’t always obvious. It happens little by little until one day Microsoft starts sending notifications.

At that point, most businesses don’t know:

  • What’s actually consuming storage
  • Which environments are growing the fastest
  • Whether growth is normal or excessive
  • If additional storage is really needed
  • How much they’re spending on storage they may not actually need

Without those answers, you’re left guessing. The good news is you don’t have to guess. enCloud9’s free Microsoft Dataverse Storage Analysis helps you understand what’s driving storage growth, identify potential risks, and create a plan before capacity issues impact your business.

Can you ignore a Dataverse storage warning?

When Dataverse storage warning notifications start showing up, it’s easy to push them to the bottom of the priority list. After all, everything is still working. But that’s exactly why these notifications catch people off guard.

Storage issues rarely become urgent overnight. They build quietly in the background until your team suddenly needs to create an environment, perform a restore, refresh a sandbox, or support a deployment. That’s when storage becomes everyone’s problem. The sooner you understand what’s driving your storage growth, the more options you’ll have to address it.

What happens when you go over your Dataverse storage limit?

Ignoring a Microsoft Dataverse storage notification doesn’t stop storage from growing. Once you go over, Microsoft’s capacity rules work in stages:

  • The day you go over: you can’t create, copy, restore or recover environments.
  • After 30 days: your sandbox environments lock to administrators only.
  • After 90 days: nobody can sign in to those sandboxes, admins included.

Your production apps keep running through all of it. But anything that needs a restore, a copy or a test environment is blocked. The longer you wait, the greater the risk of unexpected costs, unnecessary storage purchases, and operational headaches.

Frequently asked questions

Will Dynamics 365 CRM stop working?

No. Your production apps keep running. But anything that needs a restore, a copy or a test environment is blocked.

Can we turn off Microsoft’s storage warning emails or notifications?

No. Once you are near or over the limit, your admins get the emails every week and see the warning banners, with no way to switch them off.

What takes up the most Dataverse storage?

Old emails and their attachments, notes and attachments, audit history, logs, and environments nobody uses anymore. The analysis shows the tables using the most space and the ones growing fastest.
