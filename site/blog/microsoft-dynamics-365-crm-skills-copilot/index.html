In an October 8, 2026 announcement, Microsoft introduced 30 new Dynamics 365 CRM Skills designed to bring customer intelligence directly into Copilot and the flow of work, helping sales and service teams spend less time searching for information and more time serving customers. For businesses already invested in Dynamics 365 and Microsoft 365, this update offers an important look at how AI is transforming CRM from a system of record into a system of action.

The Shift from CRM as a System of Record to a System of Action

Traditionally, CRM systems have functioned as systems of record. They store customer information, sales activities, service history, and marketing engagement data. While that information is valuable, employees have often needed to leave their workflow to access it or update it.

Microsoft’s latest announcement signals a shift toward a more active role for CRM. Rather than simply storing information, Dynamics 365 can now help employees make decisions, complete tasks, and move customer interactions forward through Copilot-powered experiences.

This approach helps reduce context switching, one of the biggest productivity challenges facing modern organizations. Instead of jumping between email, Teams, CRM records, and other business applications, employees can access customer intelligence directly within the tools they use every day.

What Are the 30 New CRM Skills?

The most significant part of Microsoft’s announcement is the introduction of 30 pre-built CRM skills that connect Dynamics 365 expertise with Copilot experiences. Microsoft states these skills are built on more than 180 Dynamics 365 tools across Sales, Customer Service, and Customer Insights, providing ready-to-use capabilities without requiring organizations to build custom workflows from scratch.

For sales teams, these skills can help:

  • Analyze opportunities and identify potential risks
  • Build competitive sales strategies
  • Prepare for customer meetings and leadership reviews
  • Access customer and account context more efficiently
  • Convert meeting outcomes into suggested CRM updates and next actions

These capabilities allow sellers to spend less time on administrative work and more time focused on customer relationships and revenue-generating activities.

For customer service teams, the new skills can help:

  • Review customer and case history
  • Transform correspondence into structured case proposals
  • Research relevant answers and solutions
  • Draft customer communications
  • Surface important account information within existing workflows

Rather than searching multiple systems for customer context, service representatives can use AI-powered assistance informed by Dynamics 365 data to support faster and more informed customer interactions.

Microsoft also notes that marketers can use these skills to create consent-aware outreach and develop brand-compliant content while remaining connected to customer information and business processes.

Why This Matters for Businesses

With Copilot and CRM skills working together, customer context, recommendations and actions can appear at the moment employees need them, inside the tools they already use, instead of employees gathering information from several systems and entering updates by hand.
